# Experiment table

Every experiment I ran, in order, with what I expected, what I changed, what happened and what I decided.

CER is Whisper large-v3's character error rate on the 88-sentence benchmark (lower is better). Hinglish CER is rough: Hindi Whisper against AI-drafted Devanagari references that I checked. Sim is ECAPA speaker similarity to my reference clip in the same language. Single-seed numbers are one run (seed 1234). 3-seed numbers average seeds 1 to 3, and the brackets are a 95% bootstrap interval over sentences; a difference is real only if the interval leaves out 0. A crashed sentence counts as CER 1.0.

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis | Change | Result | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0 | Pretrained XTTS-v2, raw text | The pretrained model can read Indian text as written. | My 3 reference clips, no text processing. | 8 of 12 Hindi rows crash because XTTS's number expander fails on Hindi. CER en 0.115, hi 0.960, Hinglish 0.485 (single seed). | Raw text is unusable, so a normaliser has to come first. |
| E1 | Pretrained + normaliser | Writing numbers, ₹ and acronyms as spoken words removes the crashes and misreadings. | Numbers, ₹, dates, times and acronyms written out as spoken. | No crashes. 3 seeds: CER all 0.125, en 0.037, hi 0.198, Hinglish 0.400; sim en 0.502. | This is the baseline for every fine-tuning result. Hinglish is the worst part. |
| E2 | Fine-tune on session 1 (16 min), checkpoint by val CER | 16 minutes of my voice moves the accent and voice, and val CER can pick the checkpoint. | 1000 steps; pick the lowest val CER on 8 English and Hindi clips. | Picked step 250. Sim en 0.718 against E1's 0.497; CER Hinglish 0.311 against 0.431 (single seed). When I listened it sounded robotic and unlike me, even with the high similarity. | Failed. The pick was barely trained, and CER on 8 clips is too noisy to choose by. I changed the rule to lowest val loss with CER and duration guards. The high similarity missed the robotic sound, so ECAPA tells you who is speaking and little about how natural it sounds. |
| E2b | Session 1 only, E3's recipe | If data amount matters, E3's recipe on less data does worse than E3. | Same 2000-step target and selection rule as E3, with 208 training clips against 316. Trained twice. | Both runs picked step 1000, and val loss rises after it. 3 seeds against E3 (E3 − E2b): all +0.002 [−0.016, +0.022], en +0.008 [−0.015, +0.034], hi +0.035 [−0.003, +0.094], Hinglish −0.045 [−0.076, −0.017]. Sim en 0.721 against 0.730. Run 1, single seed: CER en 0.028, hi 0.062, Hinglish 0.263. | The extra 8 minutes, mostly Hindi and Hinglish, made a real difference only for Hinglish. English and Hindi CER can't tell 16 and 24 minutes apart. So E2 failed because of the checkpoint pick, and data amount wasn't the cause. Run 2 wasn't bit-identical to run 1, but it made the same pick, with val loss within 0.002 at that step. |
| E3 | Fine-tune on sessions 1+2 (24 min) | More data and longer training improve the voice and intelligibility over E1. | 316 train and 17 val clips, 2000 steps, val-loss rule. | Picked step 1500 (step 2000 failed the CER guard). A second run gave exactly the same selection table. 3 seeds: CER all 0.084 against E1's 0.125, −0.041 [−0.083, −0.009]; Hinglish 0.222 against 0.400; en and hi within noise; sim en 0.730. | Fine-tuning moves the voice a lot and helps Hinglish. Listeners picked it as more like me in 15 of 16 pairs. English name rows got worse (0.030 to 0.106, +0.076 [+0.005, +0.193]); all names together are within noise. |
| P7 | Hinglish as Devanagari at inference (the failure fix) | XTTS reads Hinglish badly because Roman script goes through its English path, and sending Devanagari through the Hindi path fixes it. | IndicXlit and a small word list turn Roman Hinglish into Devanagari, sent to XTTS as Hindi. | On E3: Hinglish CER 0.222 to 0.098, −0.124 [−0.177, −0.068]. On 10 held-out sentences, 0.205 to 0.119, −0.086 [−0.146, −0.034]. Listeners chose it 4 of 4. | Kept. The Devanagari step gives most of the intelligibility (the pretrained model with it scores 0.097), and fine-tuning gives the voice. |
| E4 | Train Hinglish as Devanagari | Training on Devanagari Hinglish beats converting only at inference. | Same audio, with the Hinglish transcripts converted and tagged Hindi. | Held-out Hinglish 0.126 against 0.119 for E3 with the Devanagari step, +0.007 [−0.016, +0.032]. | No gain, and IndicXlit's mistakes end up in the training text. Dropped. |
| E5 | Sampling sweep | A lower temperature or top-p cuts word errors. | Temperature 0.75, 0.5 or 0.3 with top-p 0.85 or 0.7, 3 seeds, val and held-out only. | Val CER 0.019 to 0.028, held-out Hinglish 0.107 to 0.123, and no setting was best on both. | Within noise. I kept the defaults (0.75 and 0.85). |
| E6 | Split long sentences | Splitting long sentences reduces errors on them. | Split above 120 characters with a 0.25 s gap, on 5 new long sentences. | CER en+hi 0.0088 unsplit against 0.0175 split; Hinglish 0.140 against 0.143. | It doesn't help, so it's off. |
| E7 | Name fixes at inference | Names go wrong because of sampling noise, English-style spelling or babble, so a lower temperature, a respelling list or a retry on over-long clips should fix some of them. | Temperature 0.3 on name rows; a respelling list I wrote from pronunciation before any audio was generated, covering the held-out and benchmark names; a babble guard that retries with another seed if a clip runs over 1.5 times the usual length. Tuned on 20 new held-out name sentences (AI-drafted, like the benchmark), 3 seeds. | Name words missed: base 0.34, temperature 0.39, respelling 0.46, all three 0.40. All CER differences are within noise (for example temperature −0.003 [−0.016, +0.010]). The guard never fired. | Failed for temperature and respelling, and respelling made names worse. The guard is untested, because no clip in this set ran long. Nothing was adopted, and I skipped the benchmark run because it would just repeat Final. |
| E8 | Fine-tune with name clips repeated | More exposure to names in training makes the model say them better. | E3's recipe with the 39 English training clips that contain names repeated 3 times (394 training rows), same val-only selection (picked step 2000). | Held-out names: CER 0.029 against 0.037, −0.008 [−0.022, +0.007]; name words missed 0.37 against 0.34. | No gain, so I kept Final. Repeating the same 39 clips adds no new names, so real name recordings are the next step. |
| Final | E3 + normaliser + Devanagari step | E3 with the kept fixes beats the pretrained baseline. | Default sampling, no splitting. | 3 seeds: CER all 0.061 against E1's 0.125, −0.064 [−0.110, −0.025]; Hinglish 0.098 against 0.400, −0.303 [−0.392, −0.215]; en and hi within noise. | This is the system I'm submitting. Names are its main weakness. |
